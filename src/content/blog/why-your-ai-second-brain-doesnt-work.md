It is 9 in the morning. You open your AI and you start explaining your
business. Again. Who you are, what you sell, who you sell it to, how you like
things written.

You typed all of this yesterday. And the day before that.

You built a second brain to stop doing this, and you are still doing this.

## How yours died

They all die the same way.

You read something good. You got excited. You picked a tool, maybe Notion,
maybe Obsidian, maybe a folder on your desktop called Notes. And for about 3
weeks it was beautiful. You captured everything.

You had a folder called Inbox. And one called Read Later. And one called Misc.
Misc is where systems go to die.

Then week 5 came and the pile got messy. Not messy like untidy. Messy like you
could not find anything. So you searched instead of opening, and search gave
you 40 results, and you read none of them. You stopped trusting it, so you
stopped opening it, and it went quiet.

It is still sitting there right now. You know exactly what is in it. Nothing.

Here is what you told yourself: I need more discipline. I need a better system.
I need to be more consistent. I am just not a systems person.

You were wrong about all 4.

## It was never a brain

I want to be fair to the tools first. Notion is good software. Obsidian is good
software. Neither one failed you and neither one is the problem here.

Your second brain did not die. It was never alive.

You did not build a brain. You built storage. And those are not the same thing.

Storage is a place you put things. It sits there. It waits to be opened. It
does nothing until you go and get it. That is a filing cabinet, and a good one.
Filing cabinets are useful. I own several. But a filing cabinet is not a brain.

So what is?

A brain gets walked. Something moves through it, picks up what it needs, and
knows where to go next. That something is your AI.

And here is the part nobody tells you: **your AI does not browse.** It does not
wander around looking for things. It does not get curious. It does not go
exploring. It opens what you point it at.

So a beautiful vault means nothing to it if nothing tells it where to stand.

That is the actual failure. Not your discipline. Not the app. You built a room
with no doors.

## The thing you were trying to build has a name

You may hear it called ICM. Interpretable Context Methodology. Say the long
version once and then forget it, because everybody just says ICM. A man named
Jake van Clief named it.

It is 1 idea: folders your agent can walk.

It sounds too simple to matter, so let me show you.

## 3 folders

Start with an empty folder. Make 1 folder inside it and give it a number.

`01`

Then tell it what its job is.

`01_research`

That number is not decoration. It is the order the work happens in. Research
comes first, so research is 01.

Then the next one. `02_writing`. Then `03_publishing`.

```
~/my-business/
├── 01_research/
├── 02_writing/
└── 03_publishing/
```

3 folders. That is the shape of your week. You already work this way. You just
never told your AI.

## Put something real inside

Open 02. It is empty right now, so put 1 file in it: how I like things written.

```
# How I write

Short sentences
No exclamation marks
Never sound excited about software
```

That is not a prompt. You do not paste it anywhere. It just lives in the
folder.

## Now watch the difference

Ask for something without mentioning the folder:

> Write me a post about pricing

What comes back is generic. Not wrong. Just not yours.

Now the same question with 4 extra words:

> Write me a post about pricing, using what is in 02

Same question. Same model. Same day. Different answer. Short sentences. No
exclamation marks.

**It read the folder it was standing in.**

Nobody explained anything to it. I did not paste my style guide. I pointed at a
folder, and the folder did the explaining.

## Why it does not rot

Do that 3 more times and 01 knows what you researched, 02 knows how you write,
03 knows where things get published.

None of them know about each other. That is why it keeps working.

The pile got messy because everything touched everything. 3 folders with 3 jobs
cannot pile up that way. Add a 4th when you need it and it does not disturb the
other 3.

Storage gets fuller. A brain gets bigger.

## Keep your app

The folders are the brain. Not the app they sit in.

Keep Notion. Keep Obsidian. Keep the folder on your desktop. The app was never
the problem. You just never built the brain inside it.

So go make 3 folders tonight. Number them. Give each one a job. Then point your
AI at 1 of them and watch it stop asking who you are.
